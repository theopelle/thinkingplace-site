Thinker

Al-Ghazali

Tus, Iran · about 1058 – 1111 · Persian and Sufi Poetry

Keywords: The Alchemy of Happiness, Deliverance from Error

Who he was

Al-Ghazali was born near Tus, in north-eastern Persia, and studied in Nishapur. The powerful vizier of the Seljuk Empire took him under his wing and, in 1091, made him head of the Nizamiyya in Baghdad, the most prestigious teaching post in the Muslim world.

Four years later, at the height of his fame, he went through a deep personal crisis. He gave away his wealth, left Baghdad, and spent years in Damascus, Jerusalem, and Mecca, then in quiet seclusion back home. He returned to teaching only briefly, and died in Tus in 1111.

What he cared about

Al-Ghazali was a great scholar who came to doubt that learning alone was enough. His themes:

  • Honest doubt: in his autobiography, Deliverance from Error, he describes testing every source of knowledge until he found certainty.
  • Knowing is not enough: he realised he had chosen status and ego over the inner life he wrote about.
  • The Sufi path: purifying the heart through practice, not only through study.
  • Happiness: knowing yourself is the first step to true happiness, the theme of The Alchemy of Happiness.

What might surprise you

He was a gifted philosopher who wrote a famous attack on the philosophers. His Incoherence of the Philosophers took aim at thinkers like Avicenna who built on Aristotle and Plato.

Scholars still argue about its effect. Some blame it for a later decline of science in the Islamic world; others say that decline had other causes. His contemporaries gave him the title "Proof of Islam".

Where to start reading

The Alchemy of Happiness: his own shorter Persian version of his greatest work. Claud Field's 1909 translation is free on Project Gutenberg.
