Thinker

Henry David Thoreau

Concord, United States · 1817 – 1862 · Humanists and Essayists

Keywords: Walden

Who he was

Thoreau was born in Concord, Massachusetts, the son of a pencil maker, and studied at Harvard. Back home, he became part of the circle around Ralph Waldo Emerson, the leading Transcendentalist. He earned his living in many small ways, including as a land surveyor, and filled his notebooks with close observations of nature.

In 1845 he built a small cabin on land Emerson owned, by Walden Pond, and lived there for two years and two months. He died of tuberculosis in 1862, aged 44.

What he cared about

Walden (1854) turns those years in the woods into one year, season by season. Its central ideas:

  • Live deliberately: face only the essential facts of life, and see what they teach.
  • Simplify, simplify: our lives are frittered away by detail.
  • Quiet desperation: most people, he thought, are stuck in lives they never chose.
  • Your own drummer: if you keep a different pace, perhaps you hear different music.
  • Resist injustice: a citizen may refuse to obey an unjust state.

What might surprise you

In 1846 he refused to pay a tax, in protest against slavery and the war with Mexico, and spent a night in jail. Someone, probably his aunt, paid it against his wishes. The night led to his essay "Civil Disobedience", which later inspired Tolstoy, Gandhi, and Martin Luther King Jr.

He was also an active abolitionist and helped people escaping slavery on the Underground Railroad. And his life at Walden was less cut off than the book suggests: he sometimes took his laundry home to Concord to be washed.

Where to start reading

Walden, the chapter "Where I Lived, and What I Lived For". Free on Project Gutenberg.
