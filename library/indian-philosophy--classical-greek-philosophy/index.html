Two paths

Indian Philosophy and Classical Greek Philosophy

Indian Philosophy · Classical Greek Philosophy

Both pictured the self as the driver of a chariot, and both asked whether we choose the good or the pleasant; they part on what the self finally is.

Where they agree

  • The self drives the chariot. The Katha Upanishad seats the Self in a chariot, with the intellect as driver and the mind as reins; Plato's Socrates pictures the soul as a charioteer with one noble horse and one unruly one.
  • The good is not the pleasant. In the Katha Upanishad, Death tells a boy that the good and the pleasant are two different things; Socrates says that not life, but a good life, is what we should value most.
  • Neither too much nor too little. The Gita says discipline belongs to one who eats and rests in moderation; Aristotle sees every virtue as a mean between too much and too little.

Where they part ways

  • What the self is. For the Upanishads, the deepest self is one with Brahman, the reality behind everything, and seeing this ends sorrow. For Plato, the soul has parts to put in order, and the just person is "at peace with himself".
  • Where the good life leads. Aristotle places it in this life, in the city, among citizens. The Indian texts look further: liberation from the cycle of rebirth, through self-knowledge.
