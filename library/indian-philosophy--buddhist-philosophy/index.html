Two paths

Indian Philosophy and Buddhist Philosophy

Indian Philosophy · Buddhist Philosophy

Born in the same world and sharing karma and rebirth, the two part on the self: the Upanishads look for a deep, lasting self, and Buddhism says there is none.

Where they agree

  • What we want, we become. The Brihadaranyaka Upanishad says a person is made of desires, which become will, then deeds; the Dhammapada says all that we are is the result of what we have thought.
  • Neither too much nor too little. The Gita says discipline is not for one who eats too much, or not at all; Buddhism calls its path the Middle Way.
  • The mind can be tamed. The Gita says the heart is hard to restrain but grows steady by habit; the Dhammapada holds the mind as a rider holds a wild elephant.

Where they part ways

  • The same chariot. The Katha Upanishad pictures the body as a chariot, with the Self sitting in it. In The Questions of King Milinda, the monk Nāgasena uses a chariot to make the opposite point: there is no chariot beyond its parts, and no lasting self behind a name.
  • The road to freedom. The Indian path offers several, through action, knowledge, and devotion, ending in liberation through self-knowledge. Buddhism's runs through ethics, meditation, and wisdom, to the end of suffering.
