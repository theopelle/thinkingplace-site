Path

Confucianism

Keywords: junzi, ren, ritual, filial piety, Analects, the mean

A Chinese tradition built on good character, learning, and harmony in families and society.

Where and when

Confucius (about 551–479 BCE) was born near Qufu, in the small state of Lu, in eastern China. He saw himself not as an inventor but as a transmitter of older values that his age had lost. Tradition says he taught thousands of students, and his sayings were collected by his followers in the Analects, many years after his death.

Mencius developed his ideas a century later. Suppressed under the harsh Qin dynasty, Confucianism became China's official philosophy under the Han, around 130 BCE. For centuries it was the basis of the exams that chose government officials, until they were abolished in 1905.

Key ideas

  • Humaneness (ren): kindness and care for others, the heart of a good person.
  • Ritual and good manners (li): the forms that make respect visible, from greetings to funerals.
  • The "superior person" (junzi): noble by character, not by birth.
  • Family duty: respect for parents, the root of a good society.
  • Lead by example: a ruler who acts rightly will be followed without force.

Who we read

  • Confuciusabout 551 – 479 BCE
  • Menciusabout 371 – 289 BCE

Next to its neighbours

Where Taoists step back from society, Confucians step into it, and try to make it better through learning and duty. The tradition also built rules that bound women. From the Han period on, Confucian teachers taught that a virtuous woman should follow her father, then her husband, then her sons. Early 20th-century Chinese reformers blamed Confucianism for China's troubles, but interest in it has returned since.
