Persian and Sufi Poetry and Epicureanism

Persian and Sufi Poetry · Epicureanism

Khayyam and Epicurus both say enjoy today, with little; but Epicurus seeks a mind free of longing, while the Persian poets live with it, and for the Sufis it leads towards the divine.

Where they agree

  • Today is what we have. Khayyam calls tomorrow unborn and yesterday dead; Epicurus says we don't control tomorrow, yet we put off joy.
  • A little is enough. Khayyam asks only for a book of verses, bread, wine, and the one he loves; Epicurus, for bread and water once hunger is gone. Sa'di calls contentment an immense treasure.
  • Good company. Khayyam's paradise has someone in it, singing beside him; Epicurus calls friends the greatest help to a happy life.

Where they part ways

  • Longing. Epicurus wants calm, with no storm in the soul. The Persian poets put longing at the heart of life: Rumi's reed flute sings of its lost home, and "no sickness is there like sickness of heart".
  • What lies beyond. For Epicurus, the gods, if they exist, do not interfere in human life. Khayyam finds a veil he cannot see past. For the Sufi poets, the soul longs to return to its source, and love is the way back to God.
