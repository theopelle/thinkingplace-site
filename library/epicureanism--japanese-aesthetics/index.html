Two paths

Epicureanism and Japanese Aesthetics

Epicureanism · Japanese Aesthetics

More than thirteen centuries and half the world apart, both find the good life in simple things, but they feel very differently about the fact that everything passes.

Where they agree

  • Simple things are enough. Epicurus says plain bread and water give as much pleasure as a feast; Kenkō calls a life spent chasing riches and fame foolish.
  • Don't wait to live. Epicurus warns against putting off joy; the Japanese writers pay close attention to small moments as they happen.
  • Step back from the race. Both prefer a quiet life to ambition and display.

Where they part ways

  • What passing things mean. The Epicureans accept change calmly, as atoms endlessly moving, and aim to be free of the fear it brings. The Japanese writers find beauty in it, with a gentle sadness they value rather than avoid.
  • Company. For Epicurus, friends are the greatest help to a happy life. Kenkō and Chōmei often found their peace in solitude, and in friends met through books.
