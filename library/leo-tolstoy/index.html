Thinker

Leo Tolstoy

Yasnaya Polyana, Russia · 1828 – 1910 · Existential Thought

Keywords: A Confession

Who he was

Tolstoy was born in 1828 into a family of Russian counts, on the estate of Yasnaya Polyana south of Moscow. His mother died when he was two and his father when he was nine. He left university without a degree, ran up gambling debts, and joined the army, serving as an artillery officer in the siege of Sevastopol.

In 1862 he married Sophia Behrs, and they had 13 children, eight of whom survived childhood. In the years that followed he wrote War and Peace (1869) and Anna Karenina (1878), often named among the greatest novels ever written.

Then, in his early fifties, came a crisis of meaning, and a turn to a radical, personal Christianity. He gave up meat and tried to give up his wealth. In 1910, aged 82, he set off on a train journey and died of pneumonia at a small railway station.

What he cared about

A Confession (written 1879–1880) tells how success stopped meaning anything to him, and how he searched for a reason to live. His themes:

  • The question of meaning: if death is certain, what is the point of what I do today?
  • Success is not an answer: fame, family, and wealth did not save him from despair.
  • Honesty about despair: he describes the crisis plainly, without dressing it up.
  • Faith as a way of living: he came to read the Sermon on the Mount literally, as rules for life.
  • Non-violence: never resist evil by force, a view that later shaped Gandhi and Martin Luther King Jr.

What might surprise you

The author of War and Peace came to reject his own great novels, along with most of modern Western culture. His ideas also led the Russian Orthodox Church to excommunicate him in 1901, and the Church's censors cut A Confession from the journal that first tried to print it.

His home life suffered as his views hardened. His wish to give away his wealth and the copyrights to his books caused deep conflict with his wife. He was nominated for the Nobel Prize several times and never won it.

Where to start reading

A Confession: short, direct, and personal. Aylmer Maude's English translation is free on Standard Ebooks.
