Buddhist Philosophy and Japanese Aesthetics

Buddhist Philosophy · Japanese Aesthetics

Both start from the same fact, that everything passes, and two of the Japanese writers, Chōmei and Kenkō, became Buddhist monks; but Buddhism teaches us to stop clinging, while they linger over passing things as beauty.

Where they agree

  • All things change. The Dhammapada says all created things perish; Chōmei sees people and their houses as foam on a stream.
  • Death does not wait. The Dhammapada's fool plans to stay through rain, winter, and summer, and forgets his death; Kenkō says death comes not from in front, but from behind.
  • Step away from the crowd. Chōmei and Kenkō both left public life to live as Buddhist hermits.

Where they part ways

  • What to do with passing things. For the Dhammapada, seeing that all things perish lets us bear pain without being thrown by it. Kenkō watches the full moon wane, and finds that change is what moves us.
  • Small pleasures. The Dhammapada asks the wise to give up small pleasures for a greater one. Sei Shōnagon keeps lists of them: a river boat going downstream, a drink of water in the night.
