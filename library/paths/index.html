Paths

The twelve schools of thought

  • Buddhist PhilosophyThe Buddha's teachings read as a practical philosophy: an ethics and a training of the mind to end suffering.
  • Classical Greek PhilosophyWhere Western philosophy began: asking what the world is made of, and then how we should live.
  • ConfucianismA Chinese tradition built on good character, learning, and harmony in families and society.
  • Enlightenment EthicsThe age that put reason, evidence, and freedom at the centre of how we live and govern ourselves.
  • EpicureanismA philosophy of quiet happiness: simple pleasures, good friends, and a mind free from fear.
  • Existential ThoughtWriters who faced life's hardest questions head-on: freedom, suffering, meaning, and how to live with them.
  • Humanists and EssayistsWriters who explored how to live in personal prose, short sayings, and poems, rather than in grand systems.
  • Indian PhilosophyPhilosophy from India's Hindu tradition, asking what our deepest self is, and how to act well in the world.
  • Japanese AestheticsFinding beauty in what is simple, imperfect, and passing, and paying full attention to small moments.
  • Persian and Sufi PoetryWisdom poetry from the Persian-speaking world and from Sufism, on love, longing, the present moment, and how to treat one another.
  • StoicismA practical philosophy for living well: train your judgments, act with virtue, and accept calmly what you cannot control.
  • TaoismA Chinese tradition of living in harmony with the Tao, the natural way of things, through simplicity and effortless action.
