Persian and Sufi Poetry and Buddhist Philosophy

Persian and Sufi Poetry · Buddhist Philosophy

Both see our own cravings as the real enemy and ask for a heart wiped clean; they part on where that leads: for the Buddhist texts to calm, for the Sufi poets to love.

Where they agree

  • The enemy is within. Rumi says a person ruled by cravings blames this enemy and that foe, while the real foe is his own lower self; the Dhammapada says no one but ourselves can be master of the self.
  • Clean the mirror. Rumi says a mirror reflects only once its rust is scoured away; the Dhammapada asks the wise to blow off the impurities of the self, as a smith refines silver, little by little.
  • No room for hatred. The Dhammapada says hatred ceases only by love; Sa'di, that all people are members of one another, with one common origin.

Where they part ways

  • The end of the road. Buddhism teaches that there is no fixed self, and aims at the end of suffering. For the Sufi poets, the soul longs to return to its source: Rumi pictures snow that melts into the earth and rises as mist.
  • Calm or longing. The Dhammapada likens the wise to a still lake. For Rumi, love is a sickness of the heart, and the longing itself is part of the way.
