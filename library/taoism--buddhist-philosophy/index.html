Two paths

Taoism and Buddhist Philosophy

Taoism · Buddhist Philosophy

Both say still the mind, let go, and stop clinging; the Taoists follow the natural way of things, the Buddhist texts a path out of suffering.

Where they agree

  • Still water clears. Laozi says muddy water clears if we let it be still; the Dhammapada says the wise become serene, like a deep, still lake.
  • Hold nothing. Zhuangzi's perfect person uses the mind like a mirror, which receives but does not keep; the Dhammapada says no suffering comes to one who calls nothing his own.
  • Give up winning. The Dhammapada says victory breeds hatred, and happy is the one who gives up both victory and defeat; Laozi's sage puts himself last, and is found in front.

Where they part ways

  • Where they start. Taoism starts from the Tao, the way of nature, and asks us to trust it. Buddhism starts from the unease of relying on things that do not last, and offers a way to end it.
  • Effort. The Buddhist texts call for earnestness and restraint, to make the mind an island no flood can overwhelm. The Taoists distrust effort: act without forcing, like water finding its way around a stone.
