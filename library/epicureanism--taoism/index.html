Two paths

Epicureanism and Taoism

Epicureanism · Taoism

Both praise a simple life with few desires and distrust fame and wealth; they part on company and on knowledge.

Where they agree

  • Need little. Epicurus says what nature needs is limited and easy to get, while the wealth of empty fancies has no end; Laozi asks which is dearer, fame or your life.
  • Persuade, don't force. Epicurus says nature must be persuaded, not forced; the Taoists act without forcing, like water around a stone.
  • Fame is a trap. For Epicurus, desires for fame and honours are picked up from others and never end; Laozi says whoever is content needs fear no shame.

Where they part ways

  • Company. For Epicurus, friendship is by far the most important means to a happy life, and his school lived together in a garden. The Taoists step back from society into a simpler life closer to nature.
  • Knowledge. The Epicureans studied nature to free people from fear: once we understand how the world works, Lucretius says, imagined powers lose their hold. Zhuangzi warns that life has a limit and knowledge has none, so chasing it all leads nowhere.
