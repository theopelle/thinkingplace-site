Thinker

Marcus Aurelius

Rome, Italy · 121 – 180 CE · Stoicism

Keywords: Meditations

Who he was

Marcus lost his father at three and was raised by his mother and grandfather. In 138, the emperor Hadrian chose Antoninus Pius as his heir, and Antoninus in turn adopted Marcus. As heir to the throne, Marcus studied Greek and Latin with the best teachers of his time.

He became emperor in 161 and ruled until his death in 180. It was a hard reign: wars in the East and along the Danube, and a plague that killed millions across the empire. He is remembered as the last of the "Five Good Emperors".

What he cared about

The Meditations are his private notes, written in Greek, many of them in army camps. He probably never meant them to be published. He keeps coming back to a few ideas:

  • Do your duty today, well, without waiting for thanks.
  • We are made to work together, like the hands and feet of one body.
  • Your mind is yours: things cannot disturb you, only your judgments about them.
  • Everything passes, including fame, so do not chase it.

What might surprise you

The most powerful man in the world filled his notes with reminders to be patient and humble. Yet his reign was far from peaceful, and he spent years at war.

Persecution of Christians appears to have grown in his time, though historians think he was unlikely to have ordered it. He was also succeeded by his son, Commodus, and historians still debate that choice.

Where to start reading

Meditations, Book II: short and direct, and it opens with his famous advice for the start of each day. George Long's 1862 translation is free on Project Gutenberg.
