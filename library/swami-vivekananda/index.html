Thinker

Swami Vivekananda

Calcutta (now Kolkata, India) · 1863 – 1902 · Indian Philosophy

Keywords: Karma-Yoga

Who he was

Vivekananda was born Narendranath Datta into a well-off family in Calcutta, the son of a lawyer. As a young man he was drawn to a reform movement that rejected caste barriers. At 18 he met the mystic Ramakrishna, became his closest disciple, and later took the vows of a monk.

After Ramakrishna's death he spent five years walking across India, living on alms, and saw for himself the poverty of millions under British rule. In 1893 he sailed to Chicago for the Parliament of the World's Religions. Back in India, he founded the Ramakrishna Mission in 1897, devoted to education and social service. He died in 1902, aged 39.

What he cared about

The app reads his Karma-Yoga (1896), lectures on the path of action:

  • Work without attachment: act fully, but do not cling to the results.
  • Misery comes from attachment, not from work itself.
  • Do good because it is good, not for money, fame, or reward.
  • Giving blesses the giver: in helping the world, we help ourselves.
  • Teach yourself: no one is ever really taught by another.

What might surprise you

His Chicago speech, which opened with the words "Sisters and brothers of America", called for religious tolerance and is still remembered today. He went on to lecture in the United States and England, and two American universities offered him academic posts.

He was a monk, yet he urged practical action: schools, relief work, and service to the poor. Today his birthday, 12 January, is celebrated in India as National Youth Day.

Where to start reading

Karma-Yoga, chapter 3, "The Secret of Work". Short lectures in plain English, free online.
