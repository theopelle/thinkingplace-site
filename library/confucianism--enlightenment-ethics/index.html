Two paths

Confucianism and Enlightenment Ethics

Confucianism · Enlightenment Ethics

Both build ethics on how we treat one another and how power should behave; Confucius starts from roles, ritual, and family, the Enlightenment from free individuals and their agreement.

Where they agree

  • Treat others as people. Confucius says do not do to others what you would not want done to yourself; Kant, that we must treat every person always as an end, never only as a means.
  • Power must deserve obedience. Confucius says to govern is to set things right, starting with the ruler's own conduct; for Spinoza, a person guided by reason keeps the law because they understand it, not out of fear.

Where they part ways

  • Where good government comes from. For Confucius, from the ruler's example: lead with correctness, and the people follow. For Locke and Rousseau, from an agreement among the people; Rousseau says people are born free, yet everywhere in chains.
  • Harmony or liberty. The Confucians value harmony in families and states, with duties set by each person's role. Mill says the only freedom worth the name is pursuing our own good in our own way.
  • Tradition. Confucius saw himself as passing on older values that his age had lost. The Enlightenment asked people to test beliefs by reason and evidence, not by tradition alone.
