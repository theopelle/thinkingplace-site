Two paths

Confucianism and Taoism

Confucianism · Taoism

China's classic pair, grown side by side: the Confucians build a good life through learning, ritual, and duty; the Taoists trust the natural way and doing less.

Where they agree

  • Lead without force. Confucius says a ruler whose own conduct is correct governs without giving orders; Laozi's best rulers are the ones the people barely know are there.
  • Put yourself aside. Confucius was free of obstinacy and egoism; Laozi's sage puts himself last, and is found in front.

Where they part ways

  • Learning. Confucius asks for learning with steady perseverance, and thought to go with it. Laozi turns this around: learning adds something every day, while the Tao takes something away.
  • Rules and ritual. For the Confucians, ritual and good manners make respect visible and hold families and states together. The Taoists distrust that effort, and prefer fewer rules and more trust in nature.
  • A rivalry with humour. Zhuangzi often puts Taoist words into Confucius's mouth, so that the rival school's master speaks up for stillness.
