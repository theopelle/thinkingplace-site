Thinker

George Eliot

Nuneaton, England · 1819 – 1880 · Humanists and Essayists

Keywords: Middlemarch

Who she was

George Eliot was the pen name of Mary Ann Evans, born in 1819 in Warwickshire, the daughter of an estate manager. She was a voracious reader with a religious schooling. In her twenties, friends introduced her to writers who questioned the literal truth of the Bible. As she began to doubt, her father threatened to throw her out, and she kept house for him until his death.

Her first big work was a translation of a German book on the life of Jesus. In 1850 she moved to London, where she wrote and edited for the radical Westminster Review. She also translated Spinoza's Ethics.

From 1854 she lived openly with the writer George Henry Lewes, who was married to someone else. She began writing fiction at 37, and her novels made her one of the great writers of her age. She died in 1880, months after marrying her younger friend John Cross.

What she cared about

Her novels look at ordinary people with deep attention and honesty. Middlemarch (1871–1872) is the best known. Her themes:

  • Sympathy: we live to make life less difficult for each other.
  • The weight of ordinary pain: if we truly felt all the suffering around us, we could not bear it.
  • Unhistoric acts: much of the good in the world comes from quiet lives no one records.
  • Duty and work: a good life is built from steady effort, not grand gestures.
  • Realism: fiction should show people as they are, not as fantasies.

What might surprise you

She chose a man's name partly to protect her private life. Living with a married man was a scandal, and many people cut her off. When she died, her lack of Christian faith and her life with Lewes kept her out of Westminster Abbey.

She was also a sharp critic of other women writers. Her essay "Silly Novels by Lady Novelists" (1856) mocked the empty plots of popular fiction. Virginia Woolf later called Middlemarch "one of the few English novels written for grown-up people".

Where to start reading

Middlemarch: long, but it rewards every page. Start with the Prelude and the first book. Free on Project Gutenberg.
