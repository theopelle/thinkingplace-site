The Upanishads

India · about 800 – 300 BCE and later · Indian Philosophy

Keywords: Atman, Brahman, Katha Upanishad, Isha Upanishad, Brihadaranyaka Upanishad, Mundaka Upanishad

What these texts are

The Upanishads are Sanskrit texts that close the Vedas, the oldest scriptures of Hinduism. Earlier parts of the Vedas deal with hymns and rituals; the Upanishads turn inward, to meditation, consciousness, and the nature of reality. The name is often explained as "sitting down near": a student sitting by a teacher to receive knowledge.

About 108 are known. The dozen or so oldest, called the principal Upanishads, were passed down by heart for centuries. The Brihadaranyaka is among the most ancient. The app also reads the Katha, Isha, and Mundaka, composed probably in the last few centuries BCE.

What they care about

  • The self and the whole: the deepest self (Atman) and ultimate reality (Brahman) are closely linked, and in many readings one.
  • The good and the pleasant are two different things, and the wise choose the good.
  • The self as a rider: the body is the chariot, the intellect the driver, the senses the horses.
  • We become our desires: as a person desires, so he acts, and so he becomes.
  • Seeing all beings in oneself, and oneself in all beings.

What might surprise you

They reached Europe by a long road. In 1656, the Mughal prince Dara Shukoh had fifty of them translated from Sanskrit into Persian. A French scholar then turned the Persian into Latin, and that Latin version is what Schopenhauer read. He called them the most rewarding and uplifting reading in the world.

One of the last things the poet W. B. Yeats published, in 1937, was a translation of ten principal Upanishads.

Where to start reading

The Katha Upanishad: short, and built around a story: a boy, Nachiketa, meets Yama, the god of death in Hindu tradition, and asks him about the self. F. Max Müller's translation, in the Sacred Books of the East, is free online.
