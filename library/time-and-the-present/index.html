Theme

Time and the present

Keywords: present moment, carpe diem, past and future, memory, the moment

The question in one line

Is life short, or do we waste it?

How the traditions see it

Seneca and Epictetus (Stoicism). Seneca argued that life is not short; we make it short by wasting it. How late, he said, to begin living just when we must leave. Epictetus added that he wanted to be present like the hour, and to pass like the hour.

Epicurus (Epicureanism). Epicurus warned against putting off joy for a tomorrow we do not control. The future is neither fully ours nor fully out of our hands. And remembering the good days we have had is part of a happy life.

Omar Khayyam and Hafiz (Persian and Sufi Poetry). The Persian poets feel time slipping past. Khayyam's "Moving Finger" writes and moves on, and nothing can make it go back. Their answer is to make the most of the days we have.

Kenkō and Pascal (Japanese Aesthetics, Existential Thought). Kenkō noted that the seasons come in order, but death comes when it likes, so we should not put off what matters. Pascal saw the same habit from another side: we never live, but only hope to live.

Where they agree, and where they don't

All of them say the present is the only time we truly have. They differ on what to fill it with: pleasure and wine for Khayyam, wisdom and duty for Seneca, quiet attention for Kenkō. Montaigne sums up the common ground: life is measured by its use, not its length.
