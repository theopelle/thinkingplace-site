Persian and Sufi Poetry and Stoicism

Persian and Sufi Poetry · Stoicism

Both teach patience with fortune, which gives and takes away; the Stoics meet it with reason, the Sufi poets with love.

Where they agree

  • Fortune turns. Boethius says a wheel of Fortune that stood still would not be Fortune's wheel; Hafiz, that the powers of the world endure for an hour.
  • Bear what comes. Sa'di says patience is bitter, yet it yields sweet fruit; Marcus Aurelius, that nothing happens to anyone that they are not formed by nature to bear.
  • Hardship has its use. Seneca says fire tests gold, and misfortune tests the brave; Rumi calls pain a treasure, because it holds mercies.

Where they part ways

  • Reason or the heart. The Stoics accept what comes by judging it rightly: what disturbs us is our opinion of it. For the Sufi poets, the heart does the work, and love, above all the soul's longing for God, carries a person through.
  • Calm or longing. The Stoic wants a mind free of disturbing passions. For Rumi and Hafiz, longing is part of the way, and a true lover is known by the pain of the heart.
