Thinker

Petrarch

Arezzo, Italy · 1304 – 1374 · Humanists and Essayists

Keywords: Petrarch's Secret

Who he was

Francesco Petrarca was born in Arezzo, in Tuscany, in 1304, and grew up mostly near Avignon, where the popes then lived. His father, a notary, made him study law, but he cared far more for Latin literature.

In 1327, in a church in Avignon, he saw a woman he called Laura. She became the subject of his love poems for the rest of his life, even after her death in 1348. His Latin epic Africa made him famous, and in 1341 he was crowned poet laureate in Rome.

He worked for the Church, travelled widely as a scholar and diplomat, and collected crumbling old Latin manuscripts. He spent his last years in the village of Arquà, near Padua, and died there in 1374.

What he cared about

Petrarch wanted to bring the wisdom of ancient Rome back to life and use it to live better. His themes:

  • The classics: his rediscovery of Cicero's letters helped start the Renaissance.
  • Knowing yourself: in his Secret he argues with Saint Augustine about his own failings.
  • The struggle with our faults: the pain of seeing them clearly and still not breaking free.
  • Love and longing: his sonnets to Laura became a model for poets across Europe.
  • Curiosity: he travelled for the pleasure of seeing, not only for duty.

What might surprise you

He has been called "the first tourist". In 1336 he climbed Mont Ventoux in Provence just for the view, then opened Augustine's Confessions at the top and read it as a lesson about his own soul.

He also invented the idea of the "Dark Ages" for the centuries after Rome, a label most historians now find misleading. And though his Church career meant he could not marry, he had two children, a son and a daughter, whom he later recognised.

Where to start reading

Petrarch's Secret: an imagined dialogue with Augustine, honest and searching. William H. Draper's 1911 translation is free on Project Gutenberg.
