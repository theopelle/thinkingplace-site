Text

The Dhammapada

India · sayings of the Buddha, 5th century BCE · Buddhist Philosophy

Keywords: the Buddha, the wise, Dhamma

What this text is

The Dhammapada is a collection of sayings of the Buddha in verse, and one of the most widely read Buddhist texts in the world. It has 423 short verses, grouped by theme into 26 chapters, such as thought, anger, happiness, and old age.

It belongs to the Pali Canon, the scriptures of Theravada Buddhism. Tradition holds that the Buddha spoke each verse on a particular occasion, in answer to something that had happened in his community. The title joins two words: dhamma, the teaching, and pada, path or verse.

What it cares about

  • The mind comes first: what we think shapes what we become.
  • Hatred never ends hatred; love does.
  • Self-mastery: conquering yourself is greater than conquering others.
  • Watchfulness: "earnestness" in Müller's words: stay awake, and do not drift.
  • Kindness: everyone fears pain and death, just as we do.

What might surprise you

The verses are short, but they came with stories. A later commentary, linked to the scholar Buddhaghosa, gives the event behind each verse, and it is a rich source of legends about the Buddha's life.

Many verses work by contrast: joy and suffering, good and bad action, truth and lies, side by side. The Dhammapada is a religious text, and the app reads it here as practical wisdom that anyone can test.

Where to start reading

Read a chapter at a time, in any order; the chapters on thought and on anger are good first steps. F. Max Müller's 1881 translation, made for the series Sacred Books of the East, is free on Project Gutenberg.
