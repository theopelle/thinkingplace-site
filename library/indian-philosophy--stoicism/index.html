Indian Philosophy and Stoicism

Indian Philosophy · Stoicism

Do your work and let go of the results: the Bhagavad Gita and the Stoics come strikingly close here, for different reasons.

Where they agree

  • Act, but let go of the outcome. The Gita says your business is with the action, never with its fruit; Epictetus divides things into those in our power and those that are not.
  • Your own task. The Gita says it is better to do your own task, even if you fail, than to take on another's; Marcus Aurelius asks us to love the art we have learned, poor as it may be.
  • Steady in good and bad fortune. The Gita praises the person who stays the same in honour and dishonour, heat and cold; Seneca says it matters not what you bear, but how you bear it.

Where they part ways

  • Why let go. For the Stoics, results are not up to us, and only virtue is good. For the Gita, clinging to results binds us; letting go is part of a path towards liberation.
  • Who is speaking. The Stoic teachers were people speaking to people: an emperor in his notebook, a freed slave who became a teacher. In Hindu tradition, the Gita's teaching is spoken by the god Krishna to the warrior Arjuna, on a battlefield.
