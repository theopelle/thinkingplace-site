Thinker

Fyodor Dostoevsky

Moscow, Russia · 1821 – 1881 · Existential Thought

Keywords: The Brothers Karamazov, Notes from Underground, Crime and Punishment, active love, self-deception

Who he was

Dostoevsky was born in Moscow, trained as an engineer, and made his name with his first novel, Poor Folk, in the 1840s. In 1849 he was arrested for belonging to a circle that discussed banned books critical of the Tsar. He was sentenced to death, and the sentence was changed at the very last moment.

He then spent four years in a Siberian prison camp and six more as a soldier in exile. Back in St Petersburg, he wrote the novels that made him one of the greatest writers in any language: Crime and Punishment, The Idiot, Demons, and The Brothers Karamazov.

What he cared about

Dostoevsky wrote novels, but they are full of ideas, argued by characters who live them. His main questions:

  • Freedom: people want to choose for themselves, even against their own interest.
  • Suffering: what it does to us, and whether it can have meaning.
  • Faith and doubt: he was a Russian Orthodox Christian, and his novels put that faith through hard tests.
  • Responsibility: each of us is, in some way, responsible for everyone.

What might surprise you

The moment he faced the firing squad stayed with him for life, and he wrote it into The Idiot. Later, while travelling in Western Europe, he became addicted to gambling and at times had to beg for money.

His writings also contain negative stereotypes of Jews and other groups, and he denied being an enemy of the Jews when a reader accused him of it. Nietzsche called him the only psychologist he had learned anything from.

Where to start reading

Notes from Underground (1864): short, bitter, and darkly funny, and one of the first works of existentialist literature. Constance Garnett's translation is free on Project Gutenberg.
