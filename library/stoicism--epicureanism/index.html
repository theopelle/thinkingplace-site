Two paths

Stoicism and Epicureanism

Stoicism · Epicureanism

Two schools from the same city and the same century, both after a calm mind: the Stoics through virtue, the Epicureans through pleasure, rightly understood.

Where they agree

  • Peace of mind is the aim. Both say we suffer less from events than from our fears and opinions about them.
  • Death is nothing to fear. Epictetus calls the fear of death the only terrible thing about it; Epicurus says that where death is, we are not.
  • Want little. Both see endless wanting as a trap and value a simple life.

Where they part ways

  • What makes a life good. For the Stoics, only virtue; health, wealth, and pleasure do not decide it. For the Epicureans, pleasure, meaning freedom from pain in the body and fear in the mind.
  • Public life. The Stoics expected you to serve your city and work with others. The Epicureans kept out of politics and lived among friends.
  • The world. The Stoics saw the universe as one living, ordered whole. The Epicureans saw atoms moving in empty space, with no plan behind them.
