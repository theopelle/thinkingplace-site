Two paths

Indian Philosophy and Enlightenment Ethics

Indian Philosophy · Enlightenment Ethics

Vivekananda and Kant both say do good because it is good, not for a reward; they part on what grounds that duty, and on what freedom means.

Where they agree

  • Good for its own sake. Vivekananda says let us do good because it is good to do good; Kant, that morality is not about making ourselves happy but about becoming worthy of happiness.
  • Others matter to us. Vivekananda says that in helping the world we really help ourselves; Adam Smith, that however selfish we are, the happiness of others is necessary to us.

Where they part ways

  • What grounds duty. For the Upanishads, the deepest self is one with all beings, and whoever sees the Self in all beings turns away from none of them. For Kant, duty comes from reason, which binds every rational being; for Mill, from the happiness our actions bring.
  • What freedom means. For Vivekananda, freedom comes from working without attachment to the work and its rewards. For Mill, freedom is the individual's rule over their own body and mind.
