Thinker

Confucius

Qufu, China · about 551 – 479 BCE · Confucianism

Keywords: Analects, superior man, reciprocity, the mean, ren, mean man

Who he was

Confucius, born Kong Qiu, grew up in the small state of Lu, in eastern China. His father died when he was three, and his mother raised him in poverty. He married at 19 and had a son a year later.

In 501 BCE he became governor of a town, and later Minister of Crime in Lu. Later he left his home state for years, looking for a ruler who would put his ideas into practice. He came back to Lu at 68 and spent his last years teaching; tradition speaks of three thousand students.

What he cared about

Confucius taught through conversation, and his students wrote down his sayings in the Analects. His main ideas:

  • Good character, built through learning and practice, at any age.
  • Do not do to others what you would not want done to you.
  • Family and ritual: respect, good manners, and ceremonies hold a society together.
  • Leaders as examples: a ruler who is upright needs few orders.
  • Learning as joy, shared with friends.

What might surprise you

Confucius did not see himself as an original thinker. He said he was passing on the wisdom of earlier times, which his own age had forgotten.

One ancient book, the Xunzi, tells that soon after becoming Minister of Crime he had a rival scholar executed. Whether this really happened is uncertain, but it sits uneasily with his gentle image. He also rejected violent revolution, even against rulers who had taken power by force.

Where to start reading

The Analects: short sayings and conversations with his students, collected after his death. James Legge's 1861 translation is free on Project Gutenberg.
