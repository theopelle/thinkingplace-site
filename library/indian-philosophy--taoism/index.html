Two paths

Indian Philosophy and Taoism

Indian Philosophy · Taoism

Both teach a way of acting without strain: the Gita and Vivekananda act fully but let go of the results, the Taoists act without forcing at all.

Where they agree

  • The trouble is clinging. Vivekananda says misery comes from attachment, not from work; Zhuangzi's archer shoots well for a piece of pottery but goes blind with nerves when the prize is gold.
  • Skill without strain. Zhuangzi's cook carves with his mind, not his eye, after years of practice; Vivekananda says the best work is done without any motive, for money, fame, or anything else.
  • No one simply stops. The Gita says nobody stays even an instant without acting; for the Taoists, effortless action is still action, like water finding its way around a stone.

Where they part ways

  • How hard to work. Vivekananda says work incessantly, but without attachment. Laozi says the follower of the Tao does a little less each day.
  • Duty or nature. The Gita asks each person to do their own task, their duty. The Taoists distrust duties and rules, and prefer to let things be what they are.
