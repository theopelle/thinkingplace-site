Two paths

Stoicism and Buddhist Philosophy

Stoicism · Buddhist Philosophy

Both trace our suffering to the mind rather than to events, and both treat philosophy as daily training; they part on the self and on where the training leads.

Where they agree

  • The trouble starts in the mind. Marcus Aurelius says it is our judgment about a thing that pains us, not the thing itself; the Dhammapada opens by saying that all we are is the result of what we have thought.
  • Train it every day. Seneca looked back each evening at how he had spent his day; the Dhammapada asks us to straighten a wavering thought the way a fletcher straightens an arrow.
  • Stand firm. Marcus pictures a headland that the waves break against; the Dhammapada, a rock the wind cannot shake, unmoved by blame or praise.

Where they part ways

  • The self. The Stoics look within for a reason that is truly ours, to guard and train. Buddhism says there is no fixed self behind our thoughts and feelings at all.
  • The goal. For the Stoics, virtue is the only good, lived out among others and in public life. For the Buddhist texts, the aim is the end of suffering, through ethics, meditation, and wisdom, with karma and rebirth as part of the picture.
