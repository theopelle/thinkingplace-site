Humanists and Essayists and Japanese Aesthetics

Humanists and Essayists · Japanese Aesthetics

Montaigne and Kenkō, about 250 years and half the world apart, both left public life to write short, wandering pieces about themselves, death, and small things.

Where they agree

  • Write what comes. Montaigne made himself the subject of his book; Kenkō wrote in a "follow the brush" style, letting one thought lead to the next.
  • Think of death to live better. Montaigne says that learning to die is learning to be free; Kenkō, that it is far better not to know how long life will last.
  • Friends in books. Kenkō makes friends with people long gone, by an open book and a lamp; Montaigne runs to his books to escape a troubling thought.

Where they part ways

  • Where they look for help. Montaigne drew on the ancients, and took his thoughts on death from the Stoics. Kenkō became a Buddhist monk, and impermanence is his great theme.
  • Doubt and taste. Montaigne's question is "What do I know?", and he doubts even his own certainties. Kenkō has firm views, especially on taste: a little imperfection beats perfect sameness.
