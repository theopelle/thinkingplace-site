Two paths

Indian Philosophy and Existential Thought

Indian Philosophy · Existential Thought

Schopenhauer called the Upanishads the solace of his life; beyond him, the two paths ask the same questions about freedom and meaning, and answer them very differently.

Where they agree

  • Become yourself. Tagore says we gain our freedom when we attain our truest nature; Nietzsche, "You shall become what you are."
  • Look inward. Tagore says our wants are endless until we become conscious of the soul; Schopenhauer, that what a person has in themselves matters most for their happiness.

Where they part ways

  • What "yourself" means. For Tagore, drawing on the Upanishads, our truest nature is one with all life, and with the divine. For Nietzsche, with the old certainties gone, we must create our own path and our own values.
  • Calm or fire. The Gita praises a heart not shaken by calamity, free of longing for pleasures, fear, and anger. Nietzsche asks us to be ready to burn in our own flame, so as to become new.
  • Meaning. For the Upanishads, meaning is already there, in a self waiting to be known. For these writers, meaning is not handed to us: we find it or make it, and some, like Pascal and Tolstoy, found it in faith.
