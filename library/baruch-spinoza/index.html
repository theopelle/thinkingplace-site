Thinker

Baruch Spinoza

Amsterdam, Netherlands · 1632 – 1677 · Enlightenment Ethics

Keywords: Ethics, bondage of the emotions, God or Nature

Who he was

Spinoza was born in Amsterdam into a family of Portuguese Jews who had fled to the more tolerant Dutch Republic. He received a traditional Jewish education. In 1656, at 23, the leaders of his community expelled him for his views, with the harshest ban they had ever pronounced.

From then on he belonged to no religious group. He earned his living by grinding lenses and lived quietly in Rijnsburg, Voorburg, and The Hague, surrounded by a small circle of friends. He turned down a chair of philosophy at Heidelberg, perhaps to keep his freedom of thought, and died at 44.

What he cared about

In his Ethics, set out like a geometry book, with definitions and proofs, he looks for a calm, free life:

  • God or Nature: there is only one reality, and it can be called either.
  • Bondage is being ruled by our emotions; freedom is understanding them.
  • Understanding heals: an emotion loses its grip once we see it clearly.
  • The free person thinks of life, not of death.
  • Happiness is not the reward of virtue, but virtue itself.

What might surprise you

His Theological-Political Treatise questioned the divine origin of the Bible and argued that religious authorities should have no role in a democratic state. It was banned as blasphemous, and his Ethics was published only after his death, by friends.

The lens grinding that fed him may also have helped kill him: he died of a lung disease, probably tuberculosis, possibly made worse by glass dust. Einstein later named him as the philosopher who most shaped his view of the world.

Where to start reading

The Ethics, Parts IV and V, on human bondage and freedom. Hard going, but worth it. R. H. M. Elwes's 1883 translation is free on Project Gutenberg.
