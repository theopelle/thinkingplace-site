Two paths

Buddhist Philosophy and Existential Thought

Buddhist Philosophy · Existential Thought

Both begin with suffering rather than with theory; the Buddhist texts offer a way out of it, while these writers mostly stay with it, and some learn to say yes to it.

Where they agree

  • Life is restless. Buddhism names the unease of relying on things that do not last; Pascal says we never live, but only hope to live.
  • Wanting feeds it. The Dhammapada says there is no torrent like greed; Schopenhauer, that riches and fame are like sea water: the more you drink, the thirstier you get.
  • Start from experience. The Buddha teaches a training of the mind, not a system; these writers start from real human experience, not abstract theory.

Where they part ways

  • The way out. Buddhism offers one: the Middle Way, and a tamed mind that brings happiness. These writers disagree among themselves: Pascal, Tolstoy, and Weil answered through faith, while Nietzsche chose love of fate, a yes to life as it is.
  • The self. Buddhism says there is no fixed self. Nietzsche says: become what you are, and live your own life, not the one others expect.
