Classical Greek Philosophy and Confucianism

Classical Greek Philosophy · Confucianism

Confucius died about the time Socrates was born; on opposite sides of the world, both built an ethics of character, and they part on where good character is formed.

Where they agree

  • Aim for the middle. For Aristotle, virtue is a mean between too much and too little; Confucius says going too far is as wrong as falling short.
  • Character is made by practice. We become just by doing just acts, says Aristotle; for Confucius, people start out nearly alike and grow apart by practice.
  • Know what you don't know. Socrates was wise in not thinking he knew what he didn't; Confucius calls this knowledge itself.

Where they part ways

  • Where it is learned. For Aristotle, a good life is lived in the city, among citizens who share in governing and being governed. For Confucius, it starts at home: respect for parents is the root of a good society.
  • Question or carry on. Socrates questioned everything, his own city's beliefs included. Confucius saw himself as a transmitter of older values, kept alive through ritual and good manners.
