Two paths

Indian Philosophy and Persian and Sufi Poetry

Indian Philosophy · Persian and Sufi Poetry

Both see love and devotion as a road to the divine, and both ask us to master the lower self; they part on whether the end of the road is knowing or longing.

Where they agree

  • Master yourself. The Gita says your own self is your friend, and also your enemy; al-Ghazali pictures the soul as a king, with reason as his minister and passion and anger as his officers.
  • One reality in every form. The Isha Upanishad sees the Self in all beings; Ibn Arabi's heart becomes capable of every form, a convent, a temple, and the pilgrim's Ka'ba.
  • Love as a way. Devotion is one of the Indian paths, and Tagore calls a person "a lover"; for the Sufi poets, love of God is the deepest force in a life.

Where they part ways

  • Knowing or longing. In the Upanishads, liberation comes through self-knowledge: seeing that the deepest self is one with Brahman. For the Sufis, the road runs through the heart: al-Ghazali found that reading about the Sufi path could not take him along it.
  • Calm or fire. The Gita praises a heart unshaken by calamity. For Rumi and Hafiz, love brings pain of the heart, and a true lover is known by it.
