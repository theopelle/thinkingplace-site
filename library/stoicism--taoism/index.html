Two paths

Stoicism and Taoism

Stoicism · Taoism

Both say live in agreement with nature and stop fighting what you cannot change; but for the Stoics nature means reason and duty, and for the Taoists it means yielding and doing less.

Where they agree

  • Accept what comes. Epictetus asks us to wish for things to happen as they do; Laozi praises water, which takes the low place without striving.
  • Anger is ours to drop. Epictetus says it is our own opinion that insults us; Zhuangzi notes that no one is angry with an empty boat that bumps into him.
  • Want little. Boethius, read with the Stoics, says nature is content with very little; Laozi calls discontent with one's lot the greatest calamity.

Where they part ways

  • What "nature" means. For the Stoics, nature is a rational order, so to live by nature is to live by reason. For the Taoists, the Tao is the way of things, which no name can fully hold.
  • Effort. Marcus Aurelius gets up "to the work of a human being", and the Stoics expected you to serve your city. The Taoists distrust that effort and prefer fewer rules: the follower of the Tao does a little less each day.
