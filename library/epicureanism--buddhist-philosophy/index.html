Two paths

Epicureanism and Buddhist Philosophy

Epicureanism · Buddhist Philosophy

Both say we suffer from wanting too much, and both aim at a calm mind; they part on pleasure, and on what happens when a life ends.

Where they agree

  • Sort your desires. Epicurus splits them into natural and necessary, natural only, and empty; the Dhammapada says there is no torrent like greed.
  • Choose the greater good. Epicurus passes over a pleasure when more trouble follows from it; the Dhammapada tells the wise to give up a small pleasure to see a great one.
  • Calm is the aim. Epicurus wants a body without pain and a mind without fear; the Dhammapada likens the wise to a deep, still lake.

Where they part ways

  • Pleasure. For Epicurus, pleasure rightly understood is the goal of life. The Buddhist texts aim past pleasure, at the end of craving itself: the Dhammapada calls Nirvana "the highest happiness".
  • After death. For Epicurus, the world is atoms and empty space, and when death comes, we are not. Buddhism teaches karma and rebirth: what we do shapes what follows.
