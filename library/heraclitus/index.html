Thinker

Heraclitus

Ephesus, Turkey · active about 500 BCE · Classical Greek Philosophy

Keywords: fragments, everything flows, daimon, Logos

Who he was

Heraclitus lived around 500 BCE in Ephesus, a Greek city on the coast of today's Turkey, then part of the Persian Empire. We know very little about him. Most ancient stories about his life were probably invented later from his own sayings.

One story says he gave up the hereditary title of "king" to his younger brother, which at least suggests he came from a leading family. He seems to have had little sympathy for democracy or for the crowd.

He wrote a single book, later known as On Nature, and is said to have placed it in the great temple of Artemis. The book is lost. What survives is just over a hundred short fragments quoted by other writers.

What he cared about

Heraclitus saw a world in constant change, held together by a hidden order he called the logos. His themes:

  • Everything flows: you cannot step into the same river twice, because the water is always new.
  • Opposites belong together: the way up and the way down are one and the same.
  • Harmony in strife: conflict and tension are part of how the world holds together.
  • Learning is not understanding: knowing many facts does not make you wise.
  • Look inward: "I inquired of myself", and a person's character is their guardian spirit.

What might surprise you

Even the ancients found him hard. His riddling style earned him the names "the dark" and "the obscure". He was seen as proud and gloomy, and later art paired him as "the weeping philosopher" with Democritus, "the laughing philosopher".

He was also scornful. He mocked Homer, Hesiod, and Pythagoras, along with the religious rites of his day. Yet his few lines shaped Plato, the Stoics, Hegel, Nietzsche, and Heidegger.

Where to start reading

The fragments themselves: a few pages that take a lifetime. Arthur Fairbanks's translation in The First Philosophers of Greece (1898) is free online.
