Two paths

Indian Philosophy and Confucianism

Indian Philosophy · Confucianism

Both say our duties depend on our place in life, and both prize self-mastery and example; they part on where those duties finally lead.

Where they agree

  • Your own task. The Gita says it is better to do your own task, even if you fail, than another's; Confucius cares less about having a place than about being fit for one.
  • Lead by example. The Gita says whatever a great person does, others do too; Confucius tells a man in power that if his desires are for what is good, the people will be good.
  • Neither too much nor too little. The Gita asks for food and rest in due measure; Confucius says going too far is as wrong as falling short.

Where they part ways

  • Where duty comes from. For Confucius, from relationships: parents, rulers, friends, kept up through ritual. For the Gita, from each person's own station, which in its world was tied to birth.
  • Where it leads. Confucius aims at a good person in a well-ordered society, in this life. The Indian texts look beyond it: letting go of the fruits of action is part of a path towards liberation from rebirth.
