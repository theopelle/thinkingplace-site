Thinker

Seneca

Córdoba, Spain · about 4 BCE – 65 CE · Stoicism

Keywords: Letters to Lucilius, On the Shortness of Life, On Anger

Who he was

Lucius Annaeus Seneca was born in Corduba, in Roman Spain, and grew up in Rome. He became a well-known speaker and writer. In 41 CE he was exiled to Corsica on an adultery charge that many historians doubt.

Eight years later, Agrippina brought him back to teach her son, the future emperor Nero. When Nero came to power in 54, Seneca became one of his closest advisers and, for some years, one of the most powerful men in Rome. He stepped back around 62. In 65, after a plot against Nero failed, the emperor ordered him to take his own life, although his part in the plot is unlikely.

What he cared about

For Seneca, philosophy was a cure for the mind, not a theory. He wrote less for the perfect wise person than for people still trying to improve. His key ideas:

  • Time is our only real possession, and we waste it.
  • Anger and other strong emotions come from our own judgments, so reason can calm them.
  • Death is a natural law, and thinking about it helps us live well now.
  • Each evening, look back on your day honestly, without harshness.

What might surprise you

Seneca praised a simple life while being one of the richest men in Rome, partly through money lending. Critics in his own time attacked him for it; he answered that wealth is fine if you do not depend on it.

He also served Nero, and one of his essays praises the young emperor's mercy. Readers still argue about whether he was a hypocrite or someone who saw his own weaknesses clearly. Some of his remarks about women also reflect the views of his time.

He also wrote tragedies, such as Medea and Thyestes, which later influenced Shakespeare.

Where to start reading

On the Shortness of Life: short, clear, and still sharp. Then the Letters to Lucilius, 124 letters written in his last years. Aubrey Stewart's translation is free on Project Gutenberg.
